     - To materially improve, you need features that capture YOUR taste, not
       public consensus. These are features with high true ρ to YOUR T that

       author track record, topic keywords from descriptions, your reading
       history patterns.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW THIS RELATES TO THE OLD "DROP BOTTOM X%" PLOTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The old plots asked:
  "If we discard the bottom X% by model rank, how much does average usefulness
   / enjoyment improve among the kept books?"

That is a ranking/screening question.

This new audit asks:
  "Can we show one number that means something like 'chance this book clears a
   usefulness bar'?"

That is a calibration question.

So these two can both be true at once:
  - the model is useful for screening, and the biggest gains come from removing
    a large bottom chunk
  - the model is only moderately good at calibrated absolute probabilities

This is why:
  - P(avg_usefulness >= 2.0) is recommended as the best current *single number*
  - P(top 10% usefulness within year) is still a good *secondary shortlist*
    signal if the goal is to triage very large pools

Category caveat:
  avg_usefulness >= 2.0 is an absolute bar, not category-adjusted.
  So a fiction-heavy pool can look "low" in absolute probability even if the
  model still ranks fiction books correctly relative to other fiction books.
